## 2. Search method, date range, and channels

The external search ran on 2026-08-29 after the preregistration commit. It sought works published or publicly posted no later than the same date. Historical discovery was not date-truncated.

Channels used:

- ordinary broad and exact-title scholarly web discovery;
- publisher and journal landing pages;
- DOI and bibliographic metadata records;
- arXiv records and full text;
- open journal full text and PubMed/PMC records where applicable;
- university repositories, author publication pages, and stable thesis handles;
- reference trails in primary sources and specialist reviews;
- the canonical FCP Source Register, framework packets, claim ledger, comparisons, and handoffs for reuse and duplicate control.

Search-result snippets were used only for discovery. Admission and assigned propositions required source identity plus full text or technically sufficient source text.

The three passes were:

1. `BROAD_FAMILY_AND_BOUNDARY_DISCOVERY` — historical identities, program names, core sources, reviews, and canonical overlaps;
2. `GAP_TARGETED_AND_ADVERSE_SEARCH` — dynamics, quantization, physical interpretation, empirical status, critical literature, mathematical-only cases, and missing-family checks;
3. `IDENTITY_DUPLICATE_AND_PROPOSITION_REDUNDANCY_AUDIT` — title/DOI/arXiv matching, version status, canonical-ID checks, and role-level redundancy.

The 50-candidate ledger counts distinct scientifically meaningful works considered for corpus membership. Metadata mirrors, duplicate landing pages, news items, and incidental references that never became source candidates are not counted.
