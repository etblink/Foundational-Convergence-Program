## 0. Purpose

This document establishes the first scientific baseline of the Foundational Convergence Program (FCP): the null competitor consisting of established General Relativity (GR), quantum field theory (QFT), and the Standard Model (SM), with no deeper ontology assumed.

The purpose is not to declare this framework complete or fundamental. The purpose is to record, under the common FCP protocol, what it actually assumes, derives, measures successfully, and leaves unresolved before any deeper foundational framework is compared against it.

The governing rule is:

> **Preserve results, not theories.**

No framework receives credit because it is established, and no speculative framework receives credit merely because it asks a deeper question.

---

## 1. Pre-registration boundary

### 1.1 Framework scope

For FCP-1, the null competitor is deliberately modest:

1. **General Relativity** is treated as the classical metric theory defined by the Einstein-Hilbert gravitational action with universal/minimal coupling of the metric to matter fields at the standard tested scope.
2. **Standard Model QFT** is treated as the renormalizable gauge quantum field theory with gauge structure `SU(3)_C × SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y`, the established quark/lepton and gauge-field content, the Higgs sector, Yukawa/flavour parameters, and standard perturbative/nonperturbative calculational machinery.
3. **QFT methods** include renormalization, renormalization-group evolution, perturbative amplitudes, effective-field-theory reasoning where explicitly invoked, and standard operational links to scattering/decay/transition probabilities.
4. **No deeper ontology is assumed.** FCP-1 does not select an interpretation of quantum mechanics, an ontology of fields/particles, a microscopic spacetime substrate, or a UV completion of gravity.
5. **No claim of one globally complete GR+SM dynamics is made.** The null competitor is the empirically successful patchwork of presently established theories across their tested domains.

### 1.2 Material equivalence notions

Within this baseline:

- gauge-related mathematical descriptions are not treated as distinct physical states merely because their gauge coordinates differ;
- diffeomorphism-related coordinate descriptions in GR are not treated as physically distinct merely because their coordinate labels differ;
- renormalization-scheme changes that leave all-order physical observables invariant are treated as changes of parametrization, while finite-order truncation dependence is retained as theoretical uncertainty;
- physically distinct models are not identified merely because they share a formal language such as gauge theory, action principles, or renormalization.

### 1.3 Positive evidence criterion

Positive evidence for the null competitor requires quantitative agreement of source-bound predictions/relations with observations at declared uncertainties.

### 1.4 Negative evidence criterion

A repeatable, source-qualified empirical deviation outside combined experimental and theoretical uncertainty can count against the relevant component model. A mathematical inconsistency or counterexample can defeat a claimed theorem. Because the null competitor is modular, a failure of the minimal SM need not falsify QFT generally, and a failure of GR need not falsify all geometric gravity theories.

### 1.5 Nonforcing criterion

A structure is `NONFORCED` when the declared assumptions allow multiple inequivalent choices and no source-bound theorem or empirical selector uniquely picks one.

# 3. Ten-layer common analysis

## Layer 1 — Primitive assumptions

### 3.1 GR primitives

At the FCP-1 source scope, GR assumes rather than derives:

- a four-dimensional differentiable spacetime description;
- a Lorentzian metric `g_{μν}`;
- the Einstein-Hilbert gravitational action (with any cosmological term treated as separately specified where relevant);
- universal/minimal coupling of the metric/connection to matter fields;
- Newton's constant as an empirical parameter;
- boundary/initial data appropriate to the physical problem.

The PDG gravitational review explicitly presents GR as the current standard theory of gravity, defines the Einstein gravitational Lagrangian and the universal coupling of the metric to Standard Model fields, and derives Einstein's field equations from the total action.

These assumptions are not source-derived from a deeper substrate inside the null competitor.

### 3.2 Standard Model/QFT primitives

The SM assumes rather than derives:

- local quantum-field-theoretic degrees of freedom on spacetime;
- the gauge group `SU(3)_C × SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y`;
- the corresponding gauge representations and established matter content;
- three sequential fermion families at the empirically established level;
- a complex scalar Higgs doublet in the minimal electroweak model;
- Yukawa structures/flavour mixing;
- gauge couplings and other Lagrangian parameters;
- a quantum/probabilistic measurement framework sufficient to connect amplitudes to experimentally estimated rates.

The PDG QCD review defines QCD as the `SU(3)` gauge-field component of the Standard Model and writes its Lagrangian. The PDG electroweak review defines the electroweak SM as a renormalizable `SU(2) × U(1)` gauge QFT and introduces the Higgs doublet and fermion representations.

### 3.3 What is deliberately not primitive in FCP-1

FCP-1 does **not** add:

- a preferred quantum interpretation;
- a hidden-variable ontology;
- a discrete spacetime substrate;
- a preferred cosmic initial state;
- a unique UV completion;
- a claim that the SM gauge group is mathematically inevitable;
- a claim that the observed parameter values are uniquely forced.

## Layer 2 — Derived mathematical structures

### 3.4 GR derivations

Given the GR action and coupling assumptions, the framework derives:

- Einstein's field equations;
- weak-field/Newtonian limits under appropriate approximations;
- gravitational redshift and equivalence-principle consequences;
- gravitational radiation with two tensor polarizations in linearized GR;
- strong-field compact-object predictions when the relevant solutions and initial data are supplied.

These are genuine source-derived consequences of the declared GR model, not free model choices.

### 3.5 SM/QFT derivations

Given the SM Lagrangian, field content, and parameter values/schemes, the framework derives quantitative relations among:

- scattering amplitudes and cross sections;
- decay widths and branching fractions;
- gauge-boson and Higgs interactions;
- radiative corrections;
- running couplings through renormalization-group equations;
- flavour and mixing observables;
- symmetry-breaking mass relations at the appropriate perturbative order.

The electroweak review explicitly notes that all-order physical observables are invariant under renormalization-group transport while finite-order calculations retain residual scale dependence from truncation.

### 3.6 Derivation does not mean parameter prediction

A crucial FCP distinction is retained:

> The framework can derive nontrivial relations among observables **conditional on measured/selected input parameters** without deriving the numerical values of every parameter from more primitive principles.

This distinction is central to the null baseline and will be applied equally to speculative frameworks.

## Layer 3 — Model choices and supplied inputs

The null competitor contains substantial supplied structure.

### 3.7 Empirical parameter inputs

The electroweak calculations require a chosen set of input parameters, for example combinations of `α`, `G_F`, `M_W`, `M_Z`, `M_H`, the strong coupling and flavour parameters. PDG explicitly discusses alternative electroweak input schemes.

FCP classifies the **numerical parameter values** as empirical/model inputs unless a specific relation predicts them from other measured inputs.

### 3.8 Renormalization scheme and scale

On-shell and modified-minimal-subtraction schemes are calculational/parametrization choices. The arbitrary renormalization scale appearing in `MS`-type schemes is not treated as a new physical observable. Finite-order residual scale dependence remains part of theoretical uncertainty.

### 3.9 Boundary and initial data

GR solutions require boundary/initial data and matter content. The GR field equations do not uniquely select the state of the observed universe from the equations alone.

### 3.10 Cosmological matter model

Using GR in cosmology requires a matter/energy content and cosmological initial conditions. Cold dark matter and a cosmological constant are successful phenomenological ingredients of the standard cosmological model, but the microscopic identity of most dark matter and the physical explanation for the observed vacuum-energy scale are not derived by GR+minimal SM.

## Layer 4 — Physical-realization bridges

### 3.11 GR bridge

The metric and connection are connected to observable clock rates, light propagation, orbital dynamics, gravitational-wave strain and other operationally measured quantities. This bridge is not purely formal: it is implemented through concrete timing, ranging, lensing and interferometric measurement protocols.

### 3.12 SM/QFT bridge

QFT fields and amplitudes are connected to detector-level physics through cross sections, decay rates, branching fractions, event kinematics and calibrated detector response. Renormalized masses and couplings obtain operational meaning through specified measurement/renormalization conditions.

### 3.13 Scope ceiling

FCP-1 does not claim that these successful operational bridges settle the ontology of quantum states, particles, fields, spacetime points or measurement. The null competitor is intentionally interpretation-light.

---

## Layer 5 — Dynamics

### 3.14 GR dynamics

GR supplies a classical dynamical law through its field equations, conditional on suitable initial/boundary data and matter sources. This is a genuine law-of-motion structure, not merely a set of permitted transformations.

### 3.15 SM/QFT dynamics

The SM Lagrangian defines quantum dynamics and transition amplitudes; perturbative and nonperturbative methods produce probabilities/rates for specified preparations and observables.

### 3.16 Missing unified history selector

The bounded null competitor does **not** contain a single established UV-complete dynamical law that unifies the quantum field-theoretic matter sector with dynamical spacetime at arbitrary energy/curvature.

This is recorded as an open scope boundary, not as evidence for any particular quantum-gravity alternative.

## Layer 6 — Observables

### 3.17 GR observables

Operational GR observables used in this source set include:

- differential accelerations and equivalence-principle tests;
- clock/redshift comparisons;
- orbital and pulsar timing parameters;
- gravitational-wave phase, propagation and ringdown observables.

Coordinate labels are not themselves physical observables.

### 3.18 SM/QFT observables

Operational particle-physics observables include:

- masses and widths;
- branching fractions;
- differential and inclusive cross sections;
- angular/asymmetry observables;
- coupling-sensitive rates;
- oscillation probabilities and event spectra.

QCD provides a useful warning about observability: quarks and gluons are not directly measured as free final-state partons; experimentally accessible hadronic/jet observables require factorization, hadronization and infrared-safe definitions where appropriate.

### 3.19 Completeness

The null competitor does **not** claim an exhaustive theorem that all physically meaningful observables are generated by one uniquely selected observation algebra. Its empirical success is tied to concrete operationally defined quantities.

## Layer 7 — Empirical predictions and tests

### 3.20 Electroweak precision consistency

The 2026 PDG electroweak review reports a high-precision global fit in which the measured input parameters and indirect determinations are mutually consistent at the stated fit level; no input parameter is pulled from its measured value by more than `1σ` in the cited full fit.

This is empirical credit for the specific electroweak structure and radiative-correction machinery. It is not evidence that gauge theory in general is unique.

### 3.21 QCD running

PDG combines multiple classes of determinations of `α_s` and reports a precise, stable world average together with observed energy dependence in agreement with QCD. The source explicitly describes the energy dependence of `α_s` as established in agreement with QCD.

### 3.22 Higgs sector

The 2026 PDG Higgs review reports that established Higgs couplings to gauge bosons and third-generation fermions are measured at roughly the 5–10% level in the combined Run-2 program and that the measurements summarized there are consistent with the Standard Model electroweak symmetry-breaking mechanism.

### 3.23 Neutrino oscillations expose a minimal-SM boundary

The minimal renormalizable SM as described in the PDG electroweak/neutrino reviews has massless neutrinos. Oscillation phenomenology requires nonzero mass-squared differences and nonzero mixing. Therefore the observed neutrino sector requires an extension of the minimal SM particle model.

This is an important control case:

> a highly successful framework can be empirically incomplete without the entire QFT/gauge-theory methodology being discarded.

### 3.24 GR precision and strong-field tests

The 2026 PDG gravity review records stringent equivalence-principle, solar-system, pulsar and gravitational-radiation tests, with GR passing the cited strong-field pulsar tests within measurement accuracy.

The July 2026 LVK GWTC-5 tests use 168 confident cumulative binary events and report no evidence in the released test suite requiring departure from GR at the tested scope.

This is strong empirical credit for GR across regimes actually tested. It is not a proof that GR remains exact at every energy, curvature or quantum scale.

## Layer 8 — Falsification and downgrade conditions

### 3.25 GR

GR would be empirically downgraded at a declared regime by a reproducible deviation from its quantitative predictions that survives calibration, waveform/model-systematic, environmental and statistical scrutiny.

Examples include robust violations of universal free fall, unambiguous extra gravitational-wave polarizations incompatible with GR, or reproducible propagation/dynamics inconsistent with the Einstein theory under the tested assumptions.

### 3.26 Minimal SM

The minimal SM is already known to require extension for neutrino mass. Further robust observations incompatible with every parameter choice of the minimal SM would further delimit its domain.

A discovery of a new particle or interaction does not automatically falsify QFT as a framework; it may instead require a larger QFT model.

### 3.27 Null competitor as a whole

Because the null competitor is modular and explicitly non-ToE, there is no single anomaly that automatically falsifies every element of `GR + QFT + SM`. FCP will score failures at the level of the claim actually tested.

---

## Layer 9 — Unresolved selection problems

The null competitor leaves major choices unexplained at the present baseline:

1. Why the SM gauge group and representation content take their observed form.
2. Why there are three sequential fermion generations.
3. Why Yukawa couplings and flavour/mixing parameters take their observed numerical values.
4. What mechanism generates neutrino masses and whether neutrinos are Dirac or Majorana.
5. What constitutes the dominant non-baryonic dark matter.
6. Why the cosmological constant/vacuum-energy scale is so small, or whether cosmic acceleration has another origin.
7. Which initial conditions describe the universe and whether they require a deeper selection principle.
8. How quantum fields and dynamical spacetime are unified beyond controlled low-energy/semiclassical regimes.
9. Whether any particular ontology or interpretation of quantum theory is physically selected.

These are **not** counted as failures of a claim the null competitor does not make. They are recorded as open selection/frontier problems.

## Layer 10 — Weaker-framework test

Many mathematical structures used by the null competitor are not specific enough to earn foundational convergence credit by themselves.

### 3.28 Generic or widely reproducible structures

The following are broadly generic or available in weaker frameworks:

- action/variational principles;
- gauge redundancy and quotienting;
- local symmetries;
- group representations;
- perturbation theory;
- renormalization-group flow;
- effective theories;
- conservation laws arising under specified symmetries;
- factorization/coarse-graining constructions.

FCP therefore will **not** award the null competitor special foundational credit merely because another framework also contains one of these structures.

### 3.29 What does earn empirical credit

Specific quantitative structures do earn credit when they survive precise tests, for example:

- the Einstein dynamics in tested weak- and strong-field regimes;
- QCD running and collider/hadronic predictions;
- electroweak radiative relations;
- measured Higgs interaction patterns;
- the successful three-neutrino oscillation phenomenology once the minimal SM is appropriately extended.

The empirical credit attaches to the quantitative theory/result at the tested scope, not to an unrestricted metaphysical interpretation.

# 5. Scope ceiling

The strongest defensible FCP-1 conclusion is:

> **GR + QFT + the Standard Model form an extraordinarily successful, quantitatively tested effective description of broad gravitational and particle-physics domains, but the bounded null competitor is not a Theory of Everything and does not claim to derive its own foundational arena, all of its parameters, a quantum theory of dynamical spacetime, or a unique deeper ontology.**

This conclusion has two equally important halves:

1. **Do not under-credit the null competitor.** It possesses actual dynamics, calibrated observables, precision predictions, and extensive empirical confirmation that a speculative framework must match or improve upon.
2. **Do not over-credit the null competitor.** Its empirical success does not convert every modeling input into a derived necessity, and it leaves genuine foundational and physical-frontier questions open.

No comparison against reduced NFC or another framework is authorized by this baseline itself.
